The Hook

[IMAGE "Filbert, 30" catfish Tinder card opener]

We opened with a joke that isn't really a joke: a literal catfish with a Tinder profile. Then the real number: our research found ~79% of users reported interacting with a catfish.

The question we set out to answer wasn't "how do we catch catfish" it was why does the system make catfishing so easy?

Research Mapping the Failure Chain

A weakness at every stage

[IMAGE Stage breakdown slides]

Sign-Up

Account creation takes under a minute. Onboarding asks only for age, gender, a photo, and an optional bio.

Users can spin up multiple accounts with new emails and phone numbers.

Verification

No background checks. Verification is always optional, with a 24-72 hour wait.

51.6% of users deem verification unnecessary fewer than 20% are actually verified.

Matching

41.3% were frustrated by mismatched outcomes. 43.5% saw profiles outside their expectations.

62% said matches feel random or low-quality.

Profiles

54% update photos only after a major life change. 22.5% hadn't updated in over six months.

Moderation

87% of reports are decided in under two minutes. 1 moderator per ~50,000 users.

89% of banned users return via new credentials. 60% of users abandon their complaints.

Real-World Stakes

Moderation failure isn't abstract

[IMAGE Anonymized real-world case slide]

We grounded the moderation failures in a documented real-world case: a man with multiple reported sexual-assault allegations who remained active on the app and was even algorithmically boosted into a premium visibility section long after being reported.

The case is a direct consequence of the system incentives. It isn't an edge case; it's what the current design produces at scale.

Root Cause

"The business model rewards volume, not trust."

[IMAGE "Why does Tinder have these problems?" root-cause slide]

Every weakness pointed to the same incentive: more users and faster sign-ups mean more revenue, so friction that would improve safety real verification, active moderation works against the metrics the product optimizes for.

Catfishing is the symptom. The volume-first incentive is the disease.

Future-State Design

Re-pricing trust into the product

[IMAGE Future State / verification tier slides]
  • 01 Account creation: require 2+ identity sources; use photo metadata to flag old or edited images at sign-up; new accounts earn visibility through profile quality, not by default.
  • 02 Tiered verification: facial scan + ID mandatory, cross-referenced against public databases. Visible trust tiers: no checkmark (1 ID), green (2 IDs), blue (approved).
  • 03 Recency enforcement: photo-refresh policy, engagement incentives, and activity audits to keep profiles current.
  • 04 Moderation: regional moderation weighted by severity, device/data identifiers to block returning banned users, case-tracking notifications so reporters don't give up.

Beyond Tinder

The same failure shows up everywhere

[IMAGE "Beyond Tinder: What's Next?" slide]

The volume-first failure isn't unique to dating apps it shows up wherever platforms optimize for scale over trust: vacation rentals, job boards, social platforms.

Our broader thesis: private, verified communities trade raw volume for trust, and that trade is where these categories are headed.
