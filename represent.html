Represent

Young talent and modeling agencies have no trustworthy shared place to find each other. I designed a two-sided app that serves both and it’s currently onboarding real agencies.

The Problem

Three large problems the modeling world hasn't solved

[IMAGE: Hero shot strongest app screen or device mockup]
  • 01 People who want to be models don’t know where to start, and don’t want to invest time applying when they might be rejected with no feedback.
  • 02 Agencies deal with a massive surplus of applicants arriving through multiple platforms at once managing them is chaotic and inefficient.
  • 03 Talent are wary. The line between a legitimate agency and a scam is not always visible, and that fear stops people from applying at all.

I’ve worked in the modeling industry myself, so I understand how talent presents itself and how agencies actually evaluate people. I know what it’s like to be on both sides. That firsthand knowledge shaped the product.

Designing for Two Sides

The talent side

[IMAGE: Talent-facing flow profile creation or onboarding screens]

Say you want to become a model. What do you even do? You can post on social media, DM agencies on Instagram, submit through agency websites, or wait in a casting line for hours. So many paths all time-consuming, none of them easy. And after all that effort, you still don’t know if anyone actually saw your application.

Represent collapses that into one place: build your profile once and be discoverable to every agency on the platform.

The agency side

[IMAGE: Agency-facing flow talent discovery or vetting screens]

Agencies pay in-house and freelance scouts to find candidates. Those scouts scroll through DMs, comb through website submissions, or just get lucky spotting someone in public. Either way, there’s a massive volume of applicants to sort through with no unified system.

Represent gives agencies a structured, searchable pool candidates come pre-organized, not scattered across platforms.

The trust problem

Agencies are invite-only on Represent. Scouts who want to offer contracts must do so within the app’s chat system keeping all outreach verifiable and on record.

By controlling who can reach talent and how, Represent removes the ambiguity that makes people afraid to engage in the first place.

Shipping It

Design-to-code, without a team

[IMAGE: Figma frames beside built product screens]

I designed every screen in Figma working through user flows, visual decisions, and edge cases before touching code. Then I handed those designs to Claude to build the backend and move the designs into working application code.

That pipeline let me iterate far faster than a traditional handoff would allow. A design decision made in the morning could be a working feature by the afternoon.

Outcome

Real agencies. Real traction.

[IMAGE: Live product screenshot or agency partner logos]

Represent is currently onboarding modeling agencies as early partners with more in progress.

Reflection

What I took from it

Building Represent taught me that a product starts by identifying a problem and truly understanding every stakeholder in the system. Only once I understood the real underlying issue on both sides was I able to design a solution that actually served them. The hardest part wasn’t the design it was resisting the urge to solve the surface problem before I’d found the real one.
